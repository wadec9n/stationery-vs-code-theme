# Designing a calmer editor theme

<small>by **Firstname Lastname** · {meta.readingTime}</small>

A good editor theme should _disappear_. Stationery is an attempt to push
that idea to its natural conclusion: lift the noise, keep the signal,
and let the words do the work.

<aside data-type="info">
  This post assumes some familiarity with VS Code's TextMate scopes. If you
  haven't seen them before, the [scope inspector][inspector] is the fastest way
  in.
</aside>

[inspector]: https://code.visualstudio.com/api/extension-guides/color-theme

## The brief

I started with three rules:

1. No more than **four** accent hues across the whole palette.
2. Plain prose should read like ink on paper, not like a UI.
3. Keywords get weight; values get color.

## Contrast budgeting

Accessibility guidance gives a floor (4.5:1 for body text), but it doesn't
say much about the _ceiling_. Pure black on pure white scores 21:1 — and
hurts to look at after an hour. Stationery targets **8.5:1** for body
text and lets accents drift between **4.6:1** and **6.0:1**.

```js
// Quick contrast check used during palette tuning.
import { getContrast } from "polished";

const ratio = getContrast("#3b6ea0", "#fdfaf3");
console.log(`accent vs paper: ${ratio.toFixed(2)}:1`);
```

## What surprised me

> The hardest part wasn't choosing colors — it was choosing what _not_ to
> color. — _me, three weeks in_

Operators, punctuation, and most delimiters are now token-default. The
result is a calmer field of view, especially in dense files like
`webpack.config.js` or anything generic-heavy in TypeScript.
